## What a custom theme is

Synthseek ships three themes: Synthseek, Midnight and Ocean. **Custom** is a fourth one that belongs to your account. It follows you to the devices you sign in on, and it does not change what other members see.

Open **Settings**, then **Customization**, then **Theme**, and pick **Custom**. The first time, it starts as a copy of the theme you were using. The **Custom theme** card under the picker is its editor.

A theme holds two kinds of things.

| Kind     | What it is                                                         | Where you edit it                          |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Tokens   | Named values: colors, text sizes, fonts, corner radius and shadows | **Visual**, or the files under `tokens/`   |
| Free CSS | Your own rules for parts of the app                                | **CSS**, in `components/` and `custom.css` |

## The visual editor

**Visual** lists the tokens in sections. A section opens on its essentials, **Show all** lists the rest, and the search box looks across the sections.

- A color opens your browser's color picker and keeps its opacity.
- A size or a radius is a slider.
- A font is a list of system fonts and the fonts you uploaded.
- The dot at the end of a row puts that token back to the value of the preset.

Some colors follow another one. The text step of the primary color follows the primary color, so changing the base moves it too. Set a follower by hand and it stops following. Its dot makes it follow again.

A color that carries text shows its contrast against the surface behind it, and marks a low one. A color that text sits on, such as the primary color, warns in its own row when that text gets hard to read.

**Theme options**, the menu beside the mode switch, sets the preset the theme starts from and its scheme, and holds **Share**. The scheme tells the browser to draw its own controls, such as scrollbars and menus, dark or light.

## The CSS editor

**CSS** shows the files of your theme.

| File               | What it holds                                                                                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `theme.json`       | The name, the scheme and the preset the theme started from. Synthseek writes it.                       |
| `tokens/*.css`     | One file per section of the visual editor. Synthseek reads the variables inside its `:root { }` block. |
| `components/*.css` | Your rules. A theme starts with `navigation.css`, `board.css` and `player.css`, and you can add files. |
| `custom.css`       | Your rules, loaded last.                                                                               |

The editor suggests token names after `--` and inside `var(`, and part names inside `[data-part="`. **Insert** lists the tokens, the parts and a few ready-made snippets. A problem shows under the editor with its line.

| Keys                      | What they do                           |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Tab                       | Indents                                |
| Esc, then Tab             | Moves out of the editor                |
| Ctrl+Space                | Opens the suggestions                  |
| Ctrl+Z, or Cmd+Z on a Mac | Undoes the last change, in either mode |

### What your rules can target

Target the parts listed under [Parts](#parts), and take values from the tokens, so a color you change later reaches your rules.

```css
[data-part="widget-title"] {
  letter-spacing: 0.08em;
  color: var(--color-secondary-400);
}
```

Your rules apply while **Custom** is selected. Pick another theme and they stop.

The class names you can see in the page are not meant for themes. They change between releases, and the editor warns about a rule that uses one.

### What a theme cannot load

A rule that would load something from another address is left out of your theme, and the editor marks it. That covers `@import`, and a `url()` that points anywhere else than these:

- a font you uploaded, as `fonts/<file>`
- an inline `data:` image or font
- a `#fragment` of the page

`@media`, `@supports`, `@container`, `@layer`, `@starting-style`, `@keyframes` and `@font-face` work. Other at-rules are marked and not applied.

## Fonts

**Visual**, then **Typography**, uploads a `.woff2`, `.woff`, `.ttf` or `.otf` file. It is kept with your theme and served by your own server.

An uploaded font joins the font lists under its file name, and **Use** makes it the interface font. In CSS it has the same name: `Inter.woff2` is `font-family: "Inter"`.

The name in the logo keeps its own font, whichever font tokens you set.

## Saving, drafts and preview

Your changes show as you make them, in this browser. **Save theme** stores them, and your other devices pick them up.

An unsaved draft is kept in this browser. Leave the page and come back, and the editor says it restored it. **Discard** goes back to the saved theme.

The eye button, **Preview in the app**, takes you into the app wearing the draft. The strip at the top brings you back to the editor.

A syntax error in a file turns **Save theme** off until you fix it. The bar names the file.

## History

Each file keeps its last 8 versions. **History**, in the CSS editor, lists them with their time and where each came from: Settings, a file edited on the server, a preset, or an [assistant](/ai-assistants#theme).

**Restore** loads a version into the editor. It is saved when you press **Save theme**.

## Share a theme

A theme travels as one JSON document, from **Theme options**, then **Share**.

**Export** names the theme and offers **Copy JSON** and **Download .json**. Clear **Include my free CSS** to share the tokens alone.

**Import** takes a pasted document or its file.

1. Press **Check**. Nothing changes yet. You see how many tokens it sets, which files carry free CSS, and how many remote resources were removed.
2. Tick **Tokens only** to leave your own free CSS as it is.
3. Press **Apply to draft**. The theme loads as a draft over yours.
4. Press **Save theme** to keep it, or **Discard** to go back to yours.

Free CSS can restyle or hide parts of the interface. Import the tokens alone when you do not trust where a theme came from.

Fonts do not travel in the document. The theme names the font, and whoever imports it uploads the file.

A document written by a newer Synthseek is refused. Update your server, then import it again.

## The folder on the server

A custom theme is a folder of files. In the container it lives under `/data/theme`, in a folder named after the account. [Configuration](/configuration#environment-variables) names the variable that moves it.

```text
/data/theme/<username>/
├── theme.json
├── tokens/
│   ├── brand.css
│   └── ...
├── components/
│   ├── navigation.css
│   ├── board.css
│   └── player.css
├── custom.css
└── fonts/
```

Whoever has the volume can edit these files with their own tools. Synthseek picks the change up, and **History** records it as edited on the server. A file it cannot parse is left out until it is fixed, and the rest of the theme still applies.

A character a folder name cannot hold is written as `%` and its code. Renaming an account renames its folder, and deleting an account deletes it.

## Limits

| What                                     | Limit  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------ |
| One file                                 | 256 KB |
| The files of one theme and their history | 2 MB   |
| Files in `components/`                   | 40     |
| The fonts of one account                 | 5 MB   |

When the files and their history pass 2 MB, the oldest versions go first.

Settings, an import and an assistant refuse a change that goes past a limit.

A file or a font changed on the server is not refused. One over 256 KB is left out of the theme. One that takes the theme past another limit stays in the folder and is left out too, and the editor says which. Make the theme smaller and it applies again. If the files alone pass 2 MB, or `components/` holds more than 40, Settings refuses to save until they are back under.

## If a theme locks you out

A rule can hide the controls you need to undo it. Add `?setTheme=default` to the address of your Synthseek, for example `https://music.example.com/?setTheme=default`.

Your account goes back to the Synthseek theme, and your custom theme is kept. Open **Settings**, **Customization**, **Theme**, and choose **Edit my custom theme without applying it** to fix it.

## Parts

A part is an element your CSS can target by name.

<ThemeParts />

`data-acc` is the accent a widget was given, and `data-type` is what a cover shows. Either one narrows a rule: `[data-part="media-tile"][data-acc="weekly"]`.

`data-placement` is where the player bar is drawn: `dock` at the bottom of the window, `header` in the top bar when the player is compact. A rule meant only for the bar at the bottom names it: `[data-part="player-bar"][data-placement="dock"]`.
